# CT-88 Longcab

> **Design shorthand:** An extended-cab semi-truck for space.

## Short Summary

The **Cairnworks CT-88** is a civilian long-haul combination tractor manufactured by [[Cairnworks Industrial Company]].

It should not be imagined as a conventional science-fiction freighter with a cargo bay built into the same hull.

It is a **tractor**.

The vehicle contains the crew, main navigation, primary propulsion, habitation, service systems, and the heavy structural coupling needed to pull compatible cargo assemblies. Freight is carried in detachable modules, frames, tanks, habitat sections, equipment decks, and specialist loads attached behind the tractor.

In Baseline terms, the cleanest mental model is:

**semi tractor + extended sleeper cab + deep-space utility vehicle + modular spacecraft coupling**

The specific CT-88 owned by [[Elias]] and [[Rowen]] is the extended-habitation **Longcab** configuration named [[Common Ground]].

---

## Why R-HOME Uses Tractor-and-Module Freight

R-HOME moves enormous quantities of material between extraction worlds, habitats, orbital yards, construction networks, research stations, and population centers.

A modular tractor system allows the expensive parts of a spacecraft to keep working while cargo bodies remain specialized.

The tractor does not need to become a refrigerator because it is towing food.

It does not need to become a tanker because it is moving industrial fluid.

It does not need to become a laboratory because a university needs a sealed science payload delivered.

Instead, the tractor couples to the right module and goes to work.

This makes the system flexible enough for independent transporters like [[Rowen]] while still fitting the industrial scale of R-HOME.

## Basic Physical Layout

The CT-88 should be visually readable in four major sections:

**[ cockpit ] - [ habitation cab ] - [ machinery / drive ] == [ cargo saddle ]**

### Cockpit

The forward control area has broad visibility and a working-vehicle feel.

It is not a fighter cockpit.

It is closer to the view from a commercial truck cab translated into spacecraft architecture: wide forward awareness, redundant instrumentation, route and docking displays, physical access to important controls, and enough visibility to make docking, yard movement, and close cargo work feel understandable.

A two-person working configuration is normal for the Longcab, though the family can support other crew arrangements.

### Habitation Cab

The defining feature of the **Longcab** is that habitation is not an emergency afterthought.

It is built for people who may spend large portions of their working lives aboard.

Typical Longcab spaces can include:

- a compact galley;
- common seating or a booth/table;
- private bunks or small individual sleeping rooms depending on fitout;
- shared hygiene compartment;
- clothing and personal storage;
- laundry or utility space;
- communications and personal work surfaces;
- additional cabinets and owner-installed storage.

It is still cramped by planetary-house standards.

By commercial deep-space standards, it is a home you can work from.

### Machinery / Drive Section

The rear body houses the systems that make the tractor a tractor:

- propulsion and maneuver systems;
- thermal management;
- power distribution;
- cargo-control interfaces;
- environmental support for the cab;
- diagnostics;
- service access;
- structural load paths leading into the rear coupling.

Exact drive mechanics remain intentionally undefined until R-HOME's near-light travel rules are locked.

### Cargo Saddle

The aft coupling is the visual signature of the CT-88.

It is the spacecraft equivalent of a semi's fifth wheel: a large structural interface that mechanically and electronically joins tractor and cargo assembly.

Working slang can include **the saddle** or **the fifth ring**.

A driver may say:

> **"We're saddled."**

Meaning the load is physically secured, communicating, and ready for synchronized operation.

## What the Saddle Connects

A complete coupling handshake can include:

- structural locking;
- power negotiation;
- cargo telemetry;
- navigation synchronization;
- braking / deceleration coordination;
- maneuver-thruster coordination;
- environmental monitoring where the payload requires it;
- emergency separation;
- docking and yard-control signals.

Cargo assemblies do not need to possess the full capability of a tractor.

Many can move themselves slowly around a yard, stabilize attitude, operate environmental systems, or dock under local control, but rely on a tractor for serious interplanetary or near-light transport.

This distinction is important:

**The freight module is what Rowen hauls. The CT-88 is what he drives home in.**

## Factory Family / Cab Variants

The exact model-code suffixes are not locked. The following are the current useful family shapes rather than rigid final catalog nomenclature.

### Standard Cab

A smaller commercial configuration intended for shorter route cycles, fleet work, or operations where the crew regularly returns to a station or habitat.

It can support sleeping and emergency habitation but sacrifices private space for lower mass, price, and service complexity.

### Longcab

The extended-habitation configuration.

Designed for owner-operators, two-person long-haul crews, independent contractors, remote work, and routes where the tractor itself becomes a regular residence.

[[Common Ground]] is a Longcab.

### Crewcab

A denser multi-person configuration for shift work, service teams, survey crews, or operations where additional people matter more than privacy.

It is more like a mobile work barracks than a private home.

### Heavy-Saddle / Industrial Tow Package

A reinforced structural and thermal package for unusually large, awkward, or industrially demanding loads.

It does not make the CT-88 a magic tug capable of moving anything.

It changes what loads the frame, cooling, coupler, and maneuver system are safely rated to manage.

**Exact factory names remain flexible.**

## Add-On and Cargo Module Ecosystem

The CT-88's value comes from the things it can couple to.

Cairnworks and compatible third parties can support a broad family of add-on modules.

These names are descriptive working names, not locked product codes.

### Dry Freight Spine / Container Rack

Standardized structural rack for sealed cargo containers.

The boring workhorse.

### Coldhold / Refrigerated Module

Temperature-controlled cargo body for food, biological supplies, medicines, laboratory material, or other thermally sensitive freight.

### Bulk Tank Module

Tankage for industrial fluids, processed feedstocks, gases, water, or other bulk materials appropriate to the route and containment standard.

### Equipment Flatframe

An open or partially enclosed structural deck for construction equipment, mining machinery, large fabricated components, vehicles, or irregular loads.

### Vehicle Carrier

A specialized frame for transporting smaller craft, rovers, industrial vehicles, or station service machines.

### Habitat / Personnel Pod

Temporary living or shift module used when a route requires more people than the tractor can comfortably house.

This is transport accommodation, not an excuse to turn the CT-88 into a passenger liner.

### Science / Survey Module

Shielded or instrumented payload for universities, Councils, observatories, field teams, or scientific contractors.

### Emergency Relief Module

Preconfigured food, medical, power, water, shelter, fabrication, and communications payload designed to be delivered rapidly to damaged habitats or isolated communities.

### Oversize Tow Spine

Sparse structural framework for loads that are themselves larger than a normal cargo body.

The module provides attachment geometry, control interfaces, and load distribution while leaving the cargo externally visible.

## Why the CT-88 Became Popular

The CT-88 is not the fastest tractor in R-HOME, the most luxurious, or the most advanced in every category.

Its strengths are more mundane:

- proven frame design;
- broad parts availability;
- modular service architecture;
- familiar controls;
- standardized coupling;
- long-lived aftermarket support;
- enormous conversion culture;
- owner repairability;
- good resale and secondhand viability;
- enough internal space to become personally meaningful.

This creates the same phenomenon seen with durable work vehicles in Baseline history:

once enough people own them, the ecosystem around the machine becomes almost as important as the machine itself.

## Owner-Operator Culture

The CT-88 is especially associated with people who do not experience a hard line between **work vehicle** and **personal space**.

A long-haul operator may:

- eat aboard;
- sleep aboard;
- keep clothing aboard;
- store tools aboard;
- maintain a personal entertainment library aboard;
- host a friend for a meal aboard;
- finish paperwork at the galley table;
- carry emergency supplies for somebody else's problem;
- take the tractor home and realize that home is still the tractor.

The machine gathers objects because life gathers objects.

This is the cultural niche that makes [[Common Ground]] believable.

---

## Maintenance Philosophy

CT-88s should look older in a way that communicates **continued care**, not neglect.

Common signs of age include:

- replacement hull panels from different production years;
- nonmatching service hardware;
- updated sensor packages mounted beside original housings;
- newer seals in older doors;
- old warning labels beside revised inspection markings;
- lighting replaced with whatever compliant unit was available;
- owner-built storage;
- wiring or data runs added after factory construction;
- components whose service history is longer than some careers.

A heavily modified CT-88 can be substantially better than factory specification while looking less uniform than a new ship.

**It survives because it is maintained, not because it was perfect.**

## Common Ground-Specific Fitout

The stock CT-88 entry and [[Common Ground]] should remain distinct.

The following are **Common Ground characteristics**, not universal CT-88 factory features:

- two brothers using the tractor as their regular home;
- Elias's accumulated workshop tools and old blueprints;
- Rowen using the tractor for paid freight work;
- volunteer and community work performed from the same vessel;
- family mementos stored throughout the living spaces;
- decades of brother-funded upgrades;
- mismatched repairs and personal modifications;
- the familiar galley/common table;
- the compact shared hygiene space;
- laundry equipment that has survived far longer than anyone expected;
- a replaced exterior light with a slightly different color temperature from its partner;
- the vessel name **COMMON GROUND** applied to an otherwise ordinary working tractor.

## Visual Design Rules

The CT-88 should never drift too far toward a sleek military spacecraft.

Prefer:

- industrial mass;
- believable access;
- an obvious forward cab;
- habitation windows or hints of domestic life;
- rear structural machinery;
- a huge visible cargo coupling;
- scuffs where cargo and docking work would actually create scuffs;
- layers of maintenance rather than random "space dirt";
- asymmetry created by repairs;
- warm interior light against a colder exterior.

The audience should be able to understand its purpose before anybody explains it.

**It is a truck. The road is astronomical.**

## Canon / Flexible Technical Details

### Established

- Manufacturer: [[Cairnworks Industrial Company]].
- Vehicle family: **CT-88**.
- Core role: civilian long-haul combination tractor.
- Longcab role: extended-habitation long-haul configuration.
- Distinct cargo saddle / fifth-ring concept.
- Detachable cargo-module ecosystem.
- Main tractor remains separate from whatever freight it is hauling.
- [[Common Ground]] is a CT-88 Longcab.

### Not Yet Locked

- exact dimensions;
- dry mass;
- maximum tow rating;
- exact drive classification;
- exact near-light acceleration limits;
- model production dates;
- official suffix names for Standard Cab / Longcab / Crewcab / Heavy-Saddle variants;
- exact first-party names and model numbers for cargo modules.

The draft concept art may suggest numerical specifications, but those numbers should remain **visual-development placeholders** until the R-HOME transport rules are finalized.
