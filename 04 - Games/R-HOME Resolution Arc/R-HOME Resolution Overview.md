# R-HOME Resolution Arc

## Premise

Elias and Rowen finally identify and return to [[R-HOME and Baseline|R-HOME]].

The homecoming resolves the original search but creates the most difficult political
and moral conflict of the first era.

## Do Not Make R-HOME Simply Evil

Their civilization remains recognizably human: compassionate, ambitious, fearful,
rational, political, bureaucratic, altruistic, selfish, corruptible, and capable of
sincere disagreement.

AllottaNaut's abilities could plausibly:
- evacuate populations from disasters,
- preserve endangered species and cultural works,
- contain disease,
- protect humanity from extinction,
- enable unprecedented scientific research,
- and transform travel and resource access.

Those desires are not inherently villainous.

## The Core Conflict

Many institutions initially approach AllottaNaut as an asset, strategic capability,
scientific subject, public good, or existential risk.

Elias and Rowen know AllottaNaut as a person.

The deepest issue is not "will humanity use the magic machine?"

It is:

> **Does extraordinary ability create an obligation to serve others?**

And:

> **Can a sentient being powerful enough to affect civilizations still possess the
> ordinary moral right to refuse?**

## Why Prior Arcs Matter

- [[Refuge Civilization Overview]] teaches responsibility when rescue is possible.
- The eldritch containment story teaches that dangerous life still has value.
- [[Cursed God Overview]] teaches that power cannot make every loss reversible.
- R-HOME forces those lessons into politics, consent, duty, and autonomy.

## The Meaning of Home

By this point R-HOME is no longer the brothers' only home.

They have relationships, responsibilities, artifacts, refugees, cultures, and history
inside [[The Hub]].

The original quest therefore resolves with a new question:

> **Which home?**
