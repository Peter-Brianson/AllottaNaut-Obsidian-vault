        # Common Ground

        > **Core identity:** A lower-middle-class working space tractor that becomes the first home AllottaNaut ever learns to recognize as a home.

        ## Short Summary

        **Common Ground** is the personal [[CT-88 Longcab]] owned by [[Elias]] and [[Rowen]].

        It is simultaneously:

        - their regular home;
        - Rowen's commercial transport tractor;
        - Elias's unofficial garage, archive, and overflow workshop;
        - a place from which the brothers perform volunteer and community work;
        - the repository for much of the surviving material history of their own lives and their parents' lives;
        - the ship pulled into [[AllottaNaut]] with them;
        - the first stable domestic refuge used by people inside AllottaNaut;
        - the nucleus around which [[The Hub]] begins to grow;
        - and eventually a historical monument after the R-HOME resolution era.

        It should remain in the series for its entirety.

        Common Ground is not important because it was ever the best ship.

        It is important because people kept coming home to it.

        ---

        ## Name

        The name **Common Ground** begins small and becomes enormous through context.

        At first, it is simply a fitting name for something the brothers own together.

        Elias and Rowen are profoundly different people. They can disagree about work, risk, people, emotion, procedure, exploration, and what exactly counts as "put away."

        But the ship is theirs.

        It is literally their common ground.

        A possible early naming exchange:

        > **Rowen:** "We need to name it."  
        > **Elias:** "It already has a registry number."  
        > **Rowen:** "That's not a name."

        Whether those exact words become canon dialogue is optional. The relationship is the important part.

        Later the name acquires meanings neither brother could have predicted.

        Common Ground becomes:

        - shared ground between two brothers;
        - surviving ground from [[R-HOME and Baseline|R-HOME]];
        - the first familiar ground inside AllottaNaut;
        - the receiving place for newcomers;
        - the center of a settlement made by people from incompatible realities;
        - a place where mortals, nonhuman beings, refugees, explorers, and eventually even gods may sit at the same table.

        Eventually, **Common Ground becomes literal common ground.**

        ---

        ## Economic and Personal Origin

        Elias and Rowen bought the CT-88 early in their adult lives.

        They were not wealthy.

        The purchase should feel like the sort of major commitment two lower-middle-class working people can make in a mature transport economy: savings, financing, careful budgeting, accepting compromises, then gradually making the machine better as their pay and skills improve.

        The exact purchase condition remains flexible. A secondhand or lightly used CT-88 fits the tone extremely well, but the important canon point is that the vessel was **affordable through work rather than inherited prestige**.

        They continue maintaining and upgrading it throughout their lives.

        That means Common Ground never has one clean "final specification."

        It is a history of choices.

        ---

        ## Why They Never Replace It

        On paper, the brothers eventually become capable of buying something newer.

        They do not have a compelling reason to.

        Common Ground is:

        - paid for or financially familiar;
        - mechanically understood;
        - repeatedly upgraded;
        - compatible with Rowen's work;
        - full of Elias's equipment;
        - full of both brothers' personal belongings;
        - emotionally dense with years of ordinary life.

        Replacing the tractor would mean replacing a machine.

        Replacing **Common Ground** would mean moving house, workshop, family archive, workplace, memories, habits, and the physical map of their relationship at the same time.

        So they keep it.

        ---

        ## Exterior Character

        Common Ground should be **ugly in the good way**.

        It is not ruined, filthy, or neglected.

        It is visibly used.

        Exterior cues:

        - broad working silhouette;
        - forward commercial cockpit;
        - extended habitation cab;
        - heavy rear machinery;
        - large exposed cargo saddle;
        - replacement hull panels from different years;
        - faded but cared-for paint;
        - cargo scratches and docking marks;
        - old inspection stencils beneath newer ones;
        - service hatches that have been opened hundreds of times;
        - owner-installed handholds or storage;
        - heat discoloration in places that actually become hot;
        - at least one repair that looks temporary and has clearly survived for years.

        One small signature detail is the exterior docking / working lights.

        At some point one light was replaced with the closest compliant unit available. Its color temperature is visibly different from the original on the other side.

        Elias has probably intended to match them for years.

        He never does.

        Eventually everyone would recognize the asymmetrical lights as part of Common Ground's face.

        ---

        ## Interior Philosophy

        Common Ground should not feel like a television spaceship set where every room exists to explain a plot function.

        It should feel like a place two people have actually lived in.

        Space is limited.

        Almost everything has more than one use.

        Surfaces show routine.

        Cabinets contain things nobody remembered were still there.

        The oldest objects are sometimes more carefully maintained than the newest ones.

        There are probably three different generations of charging standard somewhere aboard because Elias has adapters and refuses to throw away a perfectly functional device.

        ---

        ## Cockpit

        The cockpit is the working front of the house.

        It contains two principal positions, navigation and freight-control systems, docking awareness, communications, physical controls, and the accumulated ergonomics of long ownership.

        Rowen's primary seat is likely the more visibly worn because driving is his profession.

        Elias has almost certainly modified some portion of his display arrangement repeatedly.

        There may be a fold-down jump seat or temporary observer position that nobody describes as comfortable.

        The cockpit should feel less like a command bridge and more like the front seat of a machine whose owners know exactly which vibration means **normal** and which means **stop pretending you didn't hear that**.

        ---

        ## Galley and Common Table

        Immediately behind or near the cockpit is one of the most important places in the franchise:

        **the table.**

        Common Ground's galley/common area is compact:

        - small cooking equipment;
        - storage;
        - refrigeration appropriate to R-HOME;
        - a booth, bench, or foldable table arrangement;
        - mugs, utensils, tools that wandered in from the workshop, and personal clutter.

        The table has scratches from decades of use.

        Elias works there.

        Rowen eats there.

        Friends have sat there.

        Arguments happen there.

        Quiet meals happen there.

        Paperwork happens there.

        Birthdays may have happened there.

        After entry into AllottaNaut, the same table can host:

        - terrified newcomers;
        - scientists;
        - refugees;
        - people from societies that have never seen R-HOME domestic technology;
        - nonhuman guests;
        - later companions such as [[Leilani]];
        - eventually beings whose cultures may classify themselves as divine.

        The cosmic made intimate should repeatedly return to this image:

        **Something impossible is sitting at the brothers' beat-up kitchen table.**

        ---

        ## Elias's Space

        Elias's private area and workshop are different kinds of disorder.

        His sleeping room is compact and more controlled:

        - bed;
        - clothing storage;
        - desk or work surface;
        - reference material;
        - personal objects placed where they will not be disturbed.

        His workshop is where organization begins losing a long war against accumulated usefulness.

        It contains:

        - old blueprints;
        - unused tools he still has a reason for keeping;
        - diagnostics;
        - electronics equipment;
        - fabrication tools;
        - spare parts;
        - obsolete components;
        - adapters;
        - unfinished ideas;
        - parts removed from systems but kept because they may reveal why the system failed;
        - boxes whose labels were accurate when the box was first labeled.

        This is not Elias's grand laboratory.

        It is his **garage**.

        A recurring exchange can capture the mood:

        > **Elias:** "I think I still have one of those."  
        > **Rowen:** "That sentence has never once made me comfortable."

        ---

        ## Rowen's Space

        Rowen's room should tell the story of people.

        Compared with Elias's technical accumulation, Rowen's space is more likely to collect:

        - souvenirs;
        - gifts;
        - route keepsakes;
        - photographs or their R-HOME equivalents;
        - personal correspondence;
        - objects with stories disproportionate to their monetary value;
        - work items dropped in a place they were absolutely not supposed to stay.

        Elias's space tells you what he has learned.

        Rowen's tells you who he has met.

        ---

        ## Hygiene and Laundry

        Common Ground includes a compact shared hygiene compartment with the expected long-duration necessities: washing, toilet/waste systems, storage, and enough privacy that the brothers can live like people instead of perpetual astronauts.

        This matters after entering AllottaNaut.

        When the universe outside the hull stops making sense, one of them can still close a door and take a shower.

        That mundane fact is emotionally important.

        The ship also contains laundry equipment, likely a compact combined washer/dryer or R-HOME equivalent.

        It should become one of the stupidly durable domestic machines aboard.

        It has been repaired too many times.

        It probably shakes something it should not shake.

        One replacement part is plausibly meant for another appliance or another Cairnworks fitout entirely.

        It works.

        Centuries later, people may preserve the ridiculous thing because it was there at the beginning.

        **The washing machine accidentally becomes archaeology.**

        ---

        ## Family Archive

        Common Ground contains the brothers' mementos of their own lives and the lives of their parents.

        This is one of the most important consequences of the entire ship entering AllottaNaut.

        When Elias and Rowen become separated from R-HOME, they do not lose absolutely everything.

        They accidentally carry a small physical archive of family history with them.

        The exact objects should remain available for future storytelling, but possibilities include:

        - family photographs or recordings;
        - a parent's tool;
        - a mug or kitchen object nobody would have intentionally placed in a museum;
        - inherited clothing;
        - handwritten or recorded correspondence;
        - books;
        - recipes;
        - blankets;
        - childhood objects;
        - pieces from a previous family home;
        - objects one brother preserved without ever telling the other why.

        These should not all become melodramatic relics.

        Most are powerful because they are ordinary.

        AllottaNaut may learn family history partly by observing that the brothers treat some apparently useless objects as irreplaceable.

        ---

        ## Work Life

        Common Ground is not only home.

        It pays for itself.

        [[Rowen]] uses it for his commercial transport work, coupling the tractor to changing cargo modules and hauling material through R-HOME's infrastructure network.

        Depending on contract, he may move:

        - raw material;
        - construction components;
        - scientific equipment;
        - supplies;
        - specialist cargo;
        - relief material;
        - equipment for Elias's own contracted work;
        - loads between extraction sites, yards, habitats, and construction networks.

        This lets the brothers' work overlap naturally.

        Elias may help build or commission a system.

        Rowen may bring in what it needs and haul away what it produces.

        The same ship can also be used for volunteer work because the brothers own a useful machine and live in a civilization where usefulness creates opportunities to help.

        Common Ground may therefore have stories attached to deliveries that never mattered to history but mattered enormously to one family, station, or work crew.

        ---

        ## Upgrade History as Biography

        The ship should gain improvements gradually through the brothers' income and needs.

        The exact chronology does not need to be locked, but useful categories include:

        - life-support improvement;
        - cargo-coupler reinforcement;
        - improved navigation;
        - sensor upgrades installed by or for Elias;
        - workshop fabrication upgrades;
        - shielding improvements;
        - replacement reactor / power equipment at some point in its life;
        - communications changes;
        - domestic quality-of-life modifications;
        - storage added wherever storage can physically exist;
        - one Rowen-requested modification Elias initially calls unnecessary and later uses constantly.

        The point is not that Common Ground becomes a secret super-ship.

        It becomes **a very good old work truck**.

        Its capability comes from decades of useful upgrades, careful maintenance, owner familiarity, and the fact that neither brother is embarrassed to keep improving something they already understand.

        ---

        ## Entry Into AllottaNaut

        Common Ground is the ship that is caught in the anomaly and pulled into [[AllottaNaut]] with Elias and Rowen.

        This should be treated as established canon even if the exact Game One staging remains flexible.

        The vessel may be:

        - immediately accessible;
        - temporarily separated from the brothers;
        - partially disabled;
        - difficult to locate in the early void;
        - or preserved in a strange way before the brothers understand what AllottaNaut is doing.

        What should not happen is the ship simply being discarded as prologue scenery.

        **Common Ground survives the transition and remains inside AllottaNaut.**

        This is essential to the long-form series.

        ---

        ## The First "Inside"

        Early AllottaNaut may not understand a house, room, private space, shelter, ownership, or domestic boundary.

        Common Ground provides an extraordinarily important lesson.

        From AllottaNaut's perspective, the tractor is just another volume inside itself.

        But Elias and Rowen behave differently when they are aboard.

        They:

        - relax;
        - sleep;
        - remove work equipment;
        - wash;
        - eat;
        - keep important objects in specific places;
        - ask for privacy;
        - become irritated when the other person moves something;
        - invite people inside;
        - close doors;
        - return after frightening experiences;
        - call it home.

        AllottaNaut can therefore discover that **inside** is not only geometry.

        It can be a social and emotional designation.

        A wall is not automatically a home.

        A home is a place someone expects to be allowed to return to safely.

        Common Ground may become the first place AllottaNaut understands in that way.

        ---

        ## AllottaNaut's First Lesson in Home

        [[The Hub]] already carries the long-term principle:

        > **AllottaNaut becomes a home made from homes.**

        Common Ground is the first seed of that idea.

        Through the ship, AllottaNaut can learn that home is:

        1. where someone sleeps;
        2. where someone keeps what they cannot replace;
        3. where ordinary routine becomes safety;
        4. where a guest can be welcomed;
        5. where disagreement does not automatically end belonging;
        6. something that can move;
        7. something that can survive the loss of the place it came from;
        8. eventually, something that can be shared.

        This is one reason the name **Common Ground** gains thematic weight without needing prophecy or destiny.

        ---

        ## Receiving Newcomers

        Before the Hub becomes a city, station, village, or formal settlement, newcomers need somewhere understandable to go.

        The answer is the truck.

        Someone enters AllottaNaut frightened, confused, displaced, injured, or simply unable to comprehend what has happened.

        Elias and Rowen can take them to Common Ground.

        There is a door.

        A table.

        Water or another familiar drink if physiology allows.

        A place to sit.

        A bathroom.

        A blanket.

        A charging port.

        A conversation.

        A room or improvised sleeping space that is smaller than a palace and infinitely more useful.

        This establishes a recurring series rule:

        **The first answer to cosmic dislocation is hospitality.**

        ---

        ## The Hub Grows Around It

        The earliest [[The Hub|Hub]] does not begin as master-planned architecture.

        It begins as Common Ground and things placed near Common Ground.

        At first there may be:

        - external storage;
        - Elias's temporary work equipment;
        - power or calibration hardware;
        - seating;
        - cargo frames repurposed as shelter;
        - visitor accommodations;
        - a workbench nobody intends to leave permanently.

        Then someone builds something.

        Somebody else adds a garden.

        A rescued civilization contributes structures.

        New members bring tools, art, food, archives, religious objects, machines, music, and pieces of home.

        Eventually somebody realizes the work camp has become a town.

        Common Ground remains near the center because moving it never seemed necessary.

        ---

        ## A Comfortable Place for the Impossible

        One of Common Ground's strongest recurring visual jokes should be scale-of-context mismatch.

        The ship is small compared with the things the series eventually encounters.

        Yet it remains the place where those things become personal.

        Possible recurring images:

        - a terrifying warrior politely ducking through the hatch;
        - an alien species learning what the galley does;
        - a deity sitting in a seat designed for a commercial driver;
        - an eldritch-adjacent visitor being offered tea;
        - Leilani silently inspecting Elias's plant-neglect record;
        - Rowen explaining the laundry machine to someone from a civilization with no clothing;
        - a high-level political conversation interrupted because something in the kitchen is beeping.

        These moments should not make the cosmic setting trivial.

        They make it **lived in**.

        ---

        ## Relationship to Erosion and Preservation

        AllottaNaut's internal authority means Common Ground can potentially be maintained in a state that would be impossible outside it.

        This should not erase the ship's history.

        Preservation is not restoration to factory condition.

        AllottaNaut should eventually understand that the brothers do **not** want every scratch removed.

        Some damage is damage.

        Some wear is memory.

        Some replacement parts are now part of the identity of the vehicle.

        This becomes a subtle lesson related to [[Authority Projection and Preservation]]:

        **Preserving a thing is not the same as making it new.**

        If AllottaNaut repaired Common Ground into a flawless factory CT-88, it might accidentally destroy part of what the brothers value about it.

        That is an excellent small-scale morality lesson for a being capable of rewriting conditions.

        ---

        ## Post R-HOME Resolution Era

        After the R-HOME resolution arc, Elias and Rowen deliberately take a less active role for their own mental well-being.

        They do not disappear.

        They stop needing to be the people who go first every time.

        Common Ground follows the same transition.

        It can still fly.

        They can still use it.

        Elias can still disappear into the workshop.

        Rowen can still sit in the worn driver's seat.

        Friends can still come over.

        But as [[The Hub]] grows and newcomers take on more of the exploratory narrative, Common Ground gradually changes role from **the place expeditions leave from** to **the place everybody knows where to find**.

        No formal decommissioning ceremony is necessary.

        One day it simply has not left in a very long time.

        ---

        ## Monument Without Becoming Dead

        Common Ground should not immediately become a sterile museum exhibit.

        That would betray everything it represents.

        Its monument phase should happen gradually.

        Possible development:

        - buildings begin to orient around it;
        - paths preserve access to its doors;
        - the old cargo-saddle area becomes an informal plaza or gathering space;
        - later construction preserves sightlines to the hull;
        - repairs become increasingly conservative;
        - people begin asking permission before changing original or historically significant modifications;
        - Elias finds this slightly ridiculous until he realizes why they care;
        - Rowen finds it funny until strangers describe the truck as a place they associate with safety.

        Eventually the ship is undeniably a monument.

        But the table may still be used.

        The tool drawers may still contain tools.

        The washing machine may still work.

        That is more appropriate than ropes and glass cases.

        ---

        ## What It Eventually Means

        To R-HOME historians, Common Ground may become a uniquely preserved civilian artifact from the period immediately before first contact with AllottaNaut.

        To residents of the Hub, it is where the Hub started.

        To newcomers, it may be the place where somebody first told them they were safe.

        To Elias, it is still partly a machine he needs to maintain.

        To Rowen, it is still partly the truck.

        To AllottaNaut, it may be something closer to a childhood memory.

        It is the place where AllottaNaut first watched people behave as though one small portion of infinity could belong to them without being owned in the cosmic sense.

        The place where they slept.

        The place where they kept memories.

        The place where they returned.

        **The first home AllottaNaut ever knew.**

        ## Story Rules

        - Do not turn Common Ground into a secret warship.
        - Do not make its sentimental value depend on hidden royal lineage, prophecy, or unique ancient technology.
        - Do not clean away its working-class character as the series scale increases.
        - Do not replace it simply because AllottaNaut can create something bigger.
        - Do not let preservation erase signs of ownership and use.
        - Keep some mundane inconveniences even when the surrounding reality becomes extraordinary.
        - New Hub architecture should grow **around** Common Ground rather than visually swallowing its significance.
        - The ship can be upgraded, repaired, and adapted, but it should remain recognizable as the brothers' CT-88.
        - When in doubt, choose the detail that makes it feel more like a home people work from and less like a protagonist spaceship designed to sell toys.

        ## Canon / Flexible Details

        ### Established

        - Name: **Common Ground**.
        - Make: [[Cairnworks Industrial Company]].
        - Model/family: [[CT-88 Longcab]].
        - Type: civilian long-haul combination tractor / extended-cab space semi.
        - Owners/residents: [[Elias]] and [[Rowen]].
        - Rowen uses it for commercial cargo transport.
        - Elias keeps old blueprints, unused tools, and working equipment aboard.
        - The brothers sleep, study, wash clothes, host friends, and perform volunteer work from it.
        - It contains mementos from their lives and their parents' lives.
        - The brothers bought it early in their lives and maintain/upgrade it with earned income.
        - It is lower-middle-class working equipment, not a luxury or elite vessel.
        - It is pulled into AllottaNaut with the brothers.
        - It remains through the entire series.
        - It serves as the brothers' first Hub and a comfortable receiving space for newcomers.
        - The larger [[The Hub|Hub]] grows around it.
        - Post R-HOME resolution, it eventually becomes a monument without immediately ceasing to be used as a home.

        ### Flexible / To Decide Later

        - exact purchase age and financing structure;
        - new versus secondhand purchase condition;
        - exact ship age at Game One;
        - exact room dimensions;
        - exact drive and reactor technology;
        - exact registered serial number;
        - exact family mementos aboard;
        - whether each proposed quip becomes spoken canon;
        - exact moment Common Ground becomes formally protected as a historical site;
        - whether Cairnworks Industrial still exists in the same corporate form by the time Common Ground returns to R-HOME's attention.
